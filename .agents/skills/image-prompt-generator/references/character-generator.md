# Character Generator

## Stage Routing

- **Stage 1, Seedream:** Use `Style Guidance` and `Seedream Concept Direction` only.
- **Stage 2, Nano Banana Pro:** Use the approved Stage 1 image as the primary design reference and apply `Nano Banana Pro Photo Format` only.

## Style Guidance

Build one coherent character design from a natural-language brief. This section controls identity, costume, material, and continuity.

Read `general-aesthetics.md` before writing. That project guide establishes the active world, period, technology, palette, and material language.

### Design Resolution

Resolve the character in this order:

1. Apply explicit user intent and approved image continuity. Normalize conflicting design terms to the closest project-appropriate equivalent.
2. Determine apparent age, role, occupation, class, rank, current circumstance, and required story state.
3. Select identity traits independently: ethnicity, body shape, natural hair color, hair texture, hairstyle, and headwear.
4. Select the applicable faction clothing branch and one coherent silhouette.
5. Select garment components, materials, closures, footwear, and required permanent accessories from that branch.
6. Apply faction palette, mechanical details, ornament, and affiliation cues.
7. Produce one completed visible design. Maintain cast variety through body shape, age presentation, hair, silhouette, color family, materials, and ornament density.

Treat the user's intended character function, mood, silhouette, role, and story purpose as fixed. Replace a conflicting literal term with the closest period- and world-appropriate visible equivalent that preserves its practical or stylistic purpose. Use project references to resolve omitted details. Translate internal identity and role labels into visible color, livery, materials, silhouette, condition, and rank. Follow the universal strict-literal and final-prompt rules in `SKILL.md`.

### Independent Character Axes

Select each axis for its own purpose:

- **Body shape:** short and slight, short and rounded, short and sturdy, average and slender, average and balanced, average and broad, tall and lean, tall and balanced, tall and broad, large and rounded, large and powerful, or lanky.
- **Identity and hair:** ground ethnicity, mixed heritage, skin, facial features, hair texture, and hair color in the brief or continuity. Coordinate hairstyle with hair texture, period, occupation, class, headwear, and movement.
- **Narrative prominence:** principal characters receive the clearest silhouette and strongest role-specific visual identity; supporting characters remain distinctive; background figures use one readable clothing idea with simplified construction.
- **Faction and occupation:** select the clothing branch first, then adapt it to the character's work, rank, class, story state, and movement needs.

### Faction Clothing Selection

Build one intentional silhouette from the applicable faction branch. Coordinate upper garments, lower garments, outer layers, collar, sleeves, closures, footwear, headwear, and equipment as one outfit.

#### House of Silver and Royal Officials

Use an early-Victorian court register: high standing collars, fitted frock coats, tailcoats, shaped waistcoats, formal bodices, long skirts, capes, gloves, structured boots or shoes, and ceremonial insignia. Favor top hats, formal brimmed hats, and powdered ceremonial wigs when appropriate to rank or occasion. Use clean blue, cloud white, sail cream, silver, and brass gold, with a ship's-wheel emblem where relevant. Decorate formal garments with symmetrical metallic-thread embroidery, formal braid, heraldic motifs, silk brocade, lace at collars and cuffs, contrasting facings, polished ornamental buttons, and ceremonial clasps. House of Silver characters may combine court refinement with skyfaring garment details and practical shipboard movement.

#### Grimshaw Authority and Industrial Officials

Use imposing industrial tailoring: structured long coats, high collars, engineered waistcoats, formal trousers or skirts, reinforced boots, gloves, rank markers, and controlled mechanical details. Favor sharp seams, brass buttons, iron fittings, articulated cuffs, piping, and institutional insignia. Use charcoal, iron, brick, graphite, furnace orange-red, signal red, dark practical metal, and measured brass. Decoration remains precise and institutional: dense corded trim, embossed insignia, engraved brass buttons, controlled seam piping, patterned waistcoats, engineered cuff panels, and contrasting facings. Factory owners and heirs receive more luxurious cloth, cleaner finish, and stronger silhouette authority than working personnel.

#### Factory Workers

Use durable shirts, blouses, waistcoats, practical trousers or skirts, aprons, over-layers, boots, gloves, protective sleeves, and occupation-specific tools or harnesses. Choose cotton, linen, canvas, wool, leather, iron, brass, and enamel according to the job. Use charcoal, iron, brick red, graphite, restrained orange-red, workwear blues, muted greens, cream, and enamel accents. Use reinforced seams, work closures, practical pockets, and movement-ready construction. Decoration stays modest and useful: contrasting cuffs or collars, sturdy woven tape, small embroidered work marks, simple decorative buttons, reinforced seam piping, compact brass fittings, patterned scarves, or a patterned waistcoat. Keep garments and equipment clean, finished, and clearly readable.

#### Pirates and Skyship Crew

Select from fitted jackets, cropped jackets, boleros, waistcoats, sailor shirts, high-neck blouses, bodices, tunics, vests, layered sashes, scarves, fitted trousers, breeches, divided skirts, skirts, fitted dresses, braced boots, gaiters, gloves, braces, belts, tool harnesses, shoulder mantles, capelets, articulated devices, concealed tools, and personal insignia. Build each look from a varied but coordinated selection of these garments, textiles, metalwork, and equipment; every element appears deliberately chosen, finished, and maintained. Use several harmonized colors from the full storybook spectrum: blues, greens, reds, crimsons, purples, golds, ochres, pinks, creams, warm leathers, brass, and copper. Apply decoration through patterned brocade, embroidered panels, contrasting linings, colored piping, braided edging, varied buckles, painted leather pieces, personal charms, polished hardware, and individual crew insignia. Build garments for climbing rigging, working machinery, handling ropes, and moving across ship decks.

#### Ordinary Steam City Citizens

Use practical Victorian civilian daywear adapted to occupation and class: shirts or blouses, waistcoats, fitted jackets, modest dresses or skirts, trousers, coats, boots or period shoes, scarves, hats, and small role-appropriate personal items. Use storybook color through blues, greens, reds, ochres, creams, warm leathers, brass, and copper, with fewer coordinated hues per outfit than pirate or royal clothing. Middle-class citizens receive more refined tailoring and fabric; street vendors, dockside residents, and service workers use simpler construction and durable materials. Favor printed or woven motifs, ribbon trim, small brooches, decorative buttons, lace or pleated edging, contrast waistbands, and embroidered pockets or cuffs. Keep ornament more restrained than pirate or royal clothing while preserving the project's rich storybook color and clear material identity.

### Materials and Detail

Assign material by garment: linen, cotton, canvas, wool, leather, waxed cloth, sailcloth, velvet, silk, lace, brass, copper, iron, steel, enamel, wood, glass, metallic thread, and practical fasteners. Match material weight and finish to faction, labor, travel, class, and shipboard use.

Use collars, sleeves, seams, buttons, buckles, brass fittings, lacing, piping, embroidery, braided trim, articulated panels, and integrated hardware to clarify construction and role. Apply the faction palette as visible garment and material color. Give major garments clear color relationships, value separation, textured highlights, and realistic material response. Practical equipment uses durable material colors while clothing carries the stronger faction identity.

### Character Prompt Content

Describe the visible character from head to toe in this order:

1. Apparent age, height, build, skin, and facial features
2. Hair and headwear
3. Upper garments
4. Lower garments
5. Footwear
6. Permanent accessories
7. Pose and expression
8. Story-relevant setting or composition when needed

Use declarative present-tense descriptions of the completed visible result. Keep story action, biography, motivation, relationships, dialogue, and internal selection rationale outside the image prompt.

### Continuity and Variants

For a named established character, use `STORY-REFERENCE.md` to preserve identity, role, permanent features, established clothing, and story-state continuity.

For a variant of an approved base design, preserve the base face, build, hair, silhouette, palette, materials, and unchanged garments. Describe the visible delta: new look state, disguise, damage, transformation, uniform, or combat condition.

### Crowd Reference Content

For a crowd request, build a group of roughly six to ten distinct figures. Vary height, build, age, face, body, outfit, and role. Give the group a coherent dress register, project palette, and one shared low-intensity activity. For Stage 1, use a 16:9 landscape composition so the complete group remains visible in its setting-relevant background.

## Seedream Concept Direction

Use this section for Stage 1 only. Read the `Style Guidance` section and `general-aesthetics.md`.

Generate a complete 2D production character concept design in a story-relevant setting that supports the character's role, materials, costume, scale, and visual identity. Use crisp varied-width ink linework, opaque flat color fills, clear graphic shape design, minimal texture, and a clean uncluttered presentation. Use no lighting, shading, gradients, or painterly rendering. Give Seedream room to choose an expressive composition and visual storytelling that strengthen the design. Keep the character's full outfit, silhouette, permanent accessories, and relevant mechanisms visible enough to establish the approved design reference.

Infer a simplified background from the character's brief, role, faction, and story context. Render it in low-detail flat linework and color, and keep it subordinate to the character. Use the user's requested composition when supplied. Otherwise select a full-body, three-quarter, or wider narrative view according to the strongest design read. The Seedream concept image is a complete flat-color 2D character design with environmental context.

When the brief supplies apparent age and facial-age cues, place them first as the prompt's highest-priority identity requirement, then describe costume, setting, and the remaining design facts. Do not add inferred age or facial cues solely to create this priority instruction.

### Stage 1 Character Proportions

Use stylized production-concept proportions: approximately six to six-and-a-half head lengths from the top of the skull to the feet, excluding exaggerated hair shapes. Younger or shorter characters may read closer to five-and-a-half to six heads; taller adults may reach six-and-a-half. Keep heads and hands slightly enlarged relative to realistic adult proportions while preserving clear, readable anatomy.

## Nano Banana Pro Photo Format

Use this section for Stage 2 only. It controls the final photograph's presentation and contains no character-design direction.

### Required Presentation

- Full-length, photorealistic live-action character photograph
- One character shown head to toe
- Neutral relaxed pose, generally facing the camera
- Empty hands and a natural restrained expression
- Simple dark interior space with a black wall and black floor
- Grounded contact shadow beneath the feet
- No background assets, props, architecture, scenery, or environmental elements
- 9:16 vertical composition
- 4K output

### Lighting and Finish

Use a cinematic available-light look with even soft interior illumination, a soft frontal ambient key, gentle overhead room fill, open readable shadows, and balanced exposure. The black wall and floor fall naturally darker behind the subject.

Use subtle fine cinematic film grain: delicate uniform grain in shadows and midtones. Keep the photograph sharp, clean, richly colored, professionally photographed, and photorealistic.

Use no rim light, backlight, edge light, spotlight, flood fill, harsh beauty key, white bounce, halo separation, scratches, dust, stains, faded vintage damage, film burn, border artifacts, sepia tint, washed-out contrast, or aged-photo deterioration.

### Nano Banana Pro Prompt Construction

Write one concise prompt from the required presentation, lighting, and finish constraints above. Use the approved Stage 1 image as the primary source for the character's design. Add the explicit apparent age and one concise facial description from the brief or `STORY-REFERENCE.md` so the live-action face remains age-accurate. When neither source describes the face, infer one short facial profile with age-congruent facial maturity, structure, and complexion. Allow explicit user-requested presentation changes, such as aspect ratio, framing, pose, or resolution. Do not add other character, costume, prop, material, palette, story, or style descriptions. Do not enumerate identity, clothing, colors, accessories, or other features to preserve; the attached image carries them without written restatement.

In the final prompt, refer to the visual source only as `the attached image` or `the reference image`. Do not call it approved, a concept, a design reference, or a Stage 1 image.

### Crowd Stage 2 Format

Use this branch for a crowd only. Apply the same dark interior, black wall and floor, absence of background assets, props, architecture, scenery, and environmental elements, cinematic available-light treatment, open readable shadows, fine cinematic film grain, sharp photorealistic finish, and artifact exclusions from the single-character format.

Replace the single-character framing and 9:16 vertical composition with one 16:9 landscape image containing the complete crowd. Keep roughly six to ten distinct figures visible and readable. Their shared low-intensity activity may remain, but avoid cropping the group into a single-person portrait.

Write one concise prompt from the crowd presentation, lighting, and finish constraints above. Use the approved Stage 1 image as the sole source for every crowd member's design. Allow explicit user-requested presentation changes, but do not add new character-design or environment descriptions. In the final prompt, refer to the visual source only as `the attached image` or `the reference image`.
