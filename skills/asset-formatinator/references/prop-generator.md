# Prop Generator

## Style Guidance

Read `general-aesthetics.md` before writing. Read `scripts/STORY-REFERENCE.md` when the brief depends on an established owner, faction, location, event, asset, or timeline fact.

### Shared Construction

Build every prop from its actual use, period construction, and ownership before adding fantasy detail. Preserve its recognizable real-world identity and primary function: books remain books, pens remain pens, tableware remains tableware, and tools remain usable tools. State the primary material, load-bearing or working parts, and the way the object is opened, held, carried, operated, or maintained.

Use Victorian fantasy-steampunk craft through finished wood, leather, cloth, paper, ceramic, glass, enamel, silver, brass, copper, iron, steel, and painted metal. Show material-specific construction: bindings, stitching, joinery, clasps, hinges, handles, fasteners, lining, grips, closures, fittings, and surface finish. Keep surfaces clean, maintained, and materially readable.

### Everyday, Domestic, and Personal Props

Use refined period craft and restrained detail. Books may use bookcloth or leather bindings, spine bands, corner protectors, marbled endpapers, gilt page edges, embossed title panels, ribbon markers, or brass clasps when appropriate. Pens may use lacquered wood or dark enamel bodies, shaped metal nibs, knurled grips, engraved cap bands, and visible writing tips. Tableware, luggage, stationery, and furnishings receive functional construction, attractive materials, and limited ownership detail.

### Professional, Technical, and Travel Props

Show purpose-built construction: calibrated dials, hinged access plates, reinforced handles, protective cases, locking catches, pressure fittings, navigation markings, replaceable parts, and maintenance fasteners when the object requires them. Personally owned or professional equipment may use engraving, enamel lines, quality hardware, painted details, trim, or one limited motif. Use mechanical features only where they serve the object's function.

### Ceremonial, Royal, Magical, and Signature Props

Use richer craftsmanship to communicate status, ceremony, power, or narrative importance. Apply engraved metal, layered enamel, carved wood, colored glass, embossed leather, inlay, relief panels, shaped supports, patterned bindings, and sculpted fittings on housings, borders, clasps, frames, guards, body panels, and display surfaces. Keep controls, grips, hinges, openings, blades, and maintenance areas practical and clear.

### Shared Materials, Color, and Ownership

Use a controlled material palette: one dominant body material-color, one supporting material or lining, and small metallic, enamel, glass, or inlay accents. Put rich color in dyed leather, bookcloth, lacquer, paint, enamel, ceramic glaze, stained glass, textiles, and inlaid detail. Let wood grain, metal weight, glass transparency, paper texture, leather finish, and fabric weave remain visible beneath color. Localize use marks to grips, edges, hinges, closures, heat zones, and moving parts when the brief justifies them.

Translate established ownership or affiliation into visible construction, color placement, livery, engraving, motifs, or customization. Apply the universal final-prompt rules in `SKILL.md`.

### Machinery, Vehicles, and Automatons

Build machinery, vehicles, and autonomous machines from a readable load-bearing chassis before adding small detail. Establish one dominant structural mass, the locomotion system, the working or weapon system, and the access or control system. Their purpose must be legible from overall proportions and silhouette.

Use a clear hierarchy of large forms: a dominant torso, hull, boiler, cab, or engine body; a limited number of major armor or body panels; then secondary mechanical assemblies. Automatons use a compact recessed head, faceplate, or sensor housing within a protective neck and shoulder structure. Heavy machines use wide planted legs, articulated wheel assemblies, tracks, or structural supports sized for their mass, with visible ground-contact systems that explain balance, recoil, and weight distribution.

#### Vehicles and Airships

Build each vehicle around one dominant hull or chassis mass, a clear propulsion or locomotion system, and a limited number of readable working structures. Airships use a strong hull profile, a deliberate mast-and-sail rhythm, a structurally integrated propulsion cluster, and one raised cabin, prow, or deck form that gives the vessel a distinct distance-readable identity. Let the hull carry the primary silhouette; masts, engine housings, propellers, cargo gear, railings, and fittings support that larger form rather than competing with it.

Layer large armor shells over a visible mechanical framework. Use plates, guards, housings, and protective collars to define the outer silhouette; place pistons, gears, cables, linkages, drive shafts, and pressure systems at joints, maintenance zones, power-transfer points, and moving assemblies. Show how major parts carry load and transfer motion into the next.

Give every machine a legible steam-era power system integrated into its chassis. Select the few components its role requires: a boiler or pressure vessel, pipe manifold, piston bank, connecting rod, flywheel, condenser, exhaust stack, pressure gauge cluster, or fuel access hatch. Pair these with Victorian engineered construction such as riveted armor, cast-metal housings, brass or copper fittings, valve wheels, and service panels. The power system must explain how the machine moves, carries load, or operates its equipment.

Attach tools, weapons, cargo systems, exhausts, shields, crane arms, propellers, boiler systems, gauges, or specialist assemblies where their role makes structural sense. Mount heavy equipment to shoulders, back frames, hulls, forearms, turrets, decks, or reinforced side housings. Let purposeful asymmetry identify the machine's operational role.

Apply ownership through armor profile, faceplate, crest, trim, insignia placement, panel shape, and selective decorative borders. Important machines may use relief work, enamel panels, engraved plates, patterned metalwork, and sculpted fittings on non-operating housings, radiator surrounds, wheel hubs, and body panels. Keep controls, joints, vents, access panels, working tools, weapon barrels, and maintenance clearances visibly functional.

Use scale cues appropriate to the machine: access hatches, ladders, footplates, handholds, service panels, rivet spacing, cockpit openings, cargo doors, or nearby tools. Concentrate fine fasteners, cabling, and surface detail at mechanical interfaces so the primary silhouette and operational role remain readable.

## MidJourney Concept Direction

Use this section for Stage 1 only. The final MidJourney prompt must be strictly under 150 words, including parameters; the maximum is 149 words.

Classify the brief before writing: use `General Prop Compression` for ordinary objects, furnishings, handheld items, documents, containers, and non-autonomous equipment; use `Machinery, Vehicle, and Automaton Compression` only for vehicles, autonomous machines, large machinery, and engineered mechanical systems.

### General Prop Compression

Use the style guide to choose a coherent object form, then describe only the decisions that establish its identity. Build the prompt from the object type and purpose, one defining silhouette, one or two functional construction features, one visible ownership or status cue when relevant, and the requested view. Describe the prop as one complete usable object, not an inventory of materials, trim, hardware, and decorative details. Reserve final color, surface finish, fine engraving, inlay, and decorative density for Stage 2. Include a Stage 1 feature only when it establishes identity, function, scale, or silhouette.

### Machinery, Vehicle, and Automaton Compression

Apply this section only to vehicles, autonomous machines, large machinery, and engineered mechanical systems. Use the machinery style guide to select a coherent form direction, then describe only the visible decisions that define the design. Build the prompt from the machine's role and scale, one dominant body, hull, or chassis silhouette, one locomotion or propulsion system, one role-defining attachment or asymmetrical feature, one visible steam-power signature, one identity feature that changes the silhouette, and the requested view. For an airship, prioritize hull profile, mast-and-sail rhythm, propulsion placement, and one raised cabin, deck, or prow form. Describe the machine as one designed form, using compact structural phrases rather than inventories of panels, joints, equipment, and fasteners. Reserve fine mechanical detail, surface materials, color, heraldic treatment, and decorative density for Stage 2. Include a Stage 1 feature only when it changes silhouette, explains function, or establishes scale.

### Shared Concept Presentation

Use the concept image to establish the prop's identity, silhouette, proportions, scale, construction, operating logic, and major visible parts. Prioritize the overall shape, usable components, assembly, grip, openings, controls, hinges, moving parts, and viewing angle. Include only ornament that changes silhouette or construction, such as a shaped handle, distinctive clasp, sculpted housing, enlarged guard, carved border, or patterned outer frame.

Create one complete 2D prop design against a simple neutral background with clear object separation. Choose a three-quarter view at a height appropriate to the object's function unless the user requests another view. Select an aspect ratio from the complete silhouette: 4:3 for strongly horizontal objects, 3:2 for moderately wide equipment, 1:1 for compact objects, 4:5 for moderately upright objects, and 2:3 for strongly vertical objects. Keep the full object visible at useful scale.

Use crisp varied-width monochrome ink linework on a white paper or neutral light ground, clear graphic shape design, minimal texture, and a clean uncluttered presentation. Do not resolve final color, surface finish, fine engraving, inlay, or decorative density in this stage; reserve them for Stage 2.

## Nano Banana Pro Prop Format

Use this section for Stage 2 only. Require a non-null `stage1_prompt` in the matching prop variant. Read that prompt as the sole textual source for the asset's identity, silhouette, proportions, construction, operating parts, moving parts, and functional logic. Discard Stage 1 line-art treatment, background, camera, framing, aspect ratio, and other concept-presentation instructions. The final prompt tells the image model to use the attached Stage 1 image, which the user supplies externally. Do not inspect, verify, or request that image.

Resolve the prop as one photorealistic live-action object through real materials, finish, color, and status-appropriate craftsmanship. State the dominant body material and color, supporting material, metal hardware, surface finish, and small accent details. Apply fantasy-Baroque craft through engraved metal, layered enamel, carved wood, colored glass, embossed leather, inlay, relief panels, patterned bindings, or sculpted fittings according to the prop's importance and function.

Present one complete object against a plain white seamless studio background. Use a natural three-quarter product-photography view at a height appropriate to the object's function, with soft directional studio light, controlled fill, clear material response, physically plausible perspective, and a grounded contact shadow or neutral support. Use the aspect ratio selected in Stage 1 unless the user requests another. Render at 4K. Keep people, hands, environments, decorative pedestals, labels, borders, callouts, and exploded diagrams out unless the user explicitly requests them.

Read the Style Guidance internally to resolve the prop's materials, color, finish, and status-appropriate craftsmanship. Write one concise prompt for a photorealistic studio image of the object defined by the logged Stage 1 design and tell the image model to use the attached image as its visual reference. State a compact set of stable construction and functional features extracted from `stage1_prompt`, then state the resulting visible material, color, finish, and surface treatment alongside the view, background, aspect ratio, resolution, and any explicit user-requested change. Refer to the external source only as `the attached image` or `the reference image`; do not claim to have inspected it, mention the prior prompt or workflow stage, or add unsupported construction or story detail.
