# Environment Style Guide

## Purpose

This project-specific guide controls location identity, ownership, architecture, materials, colors, and story continuity. Read `general-aesthetics.md` before writing. Read `STORY-REFERENCE.md` when the brief names or depends on an established location, faction, asset, event, or story state.

## Location and Faction Selection

### Steam City and Grimshaw Industry

Build the city in purposeful vertical layers: paved market and loading streets below, raised rail lines and bridge-linked walkways above, dock platforms projecting from tower faces, roof service decks, and skyship berths reached by cargo lifts, cranes, gangways, and stair towers. Give each level a legible function, access route, and structural relationship to the levels around it.

Combine stone, iron, brick, colored glass, ceramic, and engineered metal into distinctive landmark silhouettes rather than an even field of factories. Use tall civic or industrial towers, elongated arched facade bays, suspended docks, bridge spans, domes, roof lanterns, large integrated gears, and navigation or timing mechanisms to shape the skyline. Give public-facing structures flowing wrought-metal rails and brackets, sinuous Art Nouveau-inspired geometry, patterned glass, sculpted facade panels, and ornamental metalwork that grows from the structure's function.

Pipes, piston housings, winches, pressure systems, and cargo machinery must emerge from, support, or pass through the architecture; make them part of the building's construction and work flow. Use charcoal, iron, graphite, measured brass, enamel, painted metal, stone, colored glass, and selective brick as material color families rather than making red brick the city's default surface. Grimshaw-controlled spaces are precise, imposing, and materially controlled, with repeated structural bays and carefully organized industrial systems.

### House of Silver and Royal Skyfaring Legacy

Use ordered, ceremonial structures shaped by flight and navigation: refined towers, elevated terraces, formal docks, observatory-like chambers, airy bridges, domes, and carefully proportioned facades. Integrate ship's-wheel geometry, silver details, brass-gold trim, blue-and-white sailcloth, colored glass, carved stone, and maintained timber into the building fabric. Make large mechanical or navigational devices read as civic emblems and inherited craft, with selective ornament and clean material care rather than exposed industrial clutter.

### Pirate Skyships, Ports, and Crew-Owned Spaces

Use maintained timber hulls, masts, rigging, sails, brass fittings, customized deck equipment, cargo areas, climbing routes, and practical shipboard mechanisms. Build interest through varied decks, rail heights, cabin additions, rope paths, removable cargo gear, and hand-fitted repairs that still look deliberate and capable. Apply several coordinated storybook colors through sailcloth, paint, enamel, canvas, glass, and personal crew marks while preserving the vessel's nautical function.

### Floating Island and Ancient Royal Sites

Use aerial geography with navigable landforms, terraces, bridges, landing approaches, and crafted remnants that clarify how people reach, cross, and occupy the site. Pair natural rock, vegetation, cloud, and water systems with deliberate royal structures, ship's-wheel geometry, blue energy, blue-white linework, carved stone, colored glass, and preserved mechanical devices. Keep a clear relationship between natural formation and constructed legacy; scale and exact architecture remain responsive to the brief.

### Open Sky, Islands, and Unaffiliated Places

Use terrain, weather, vegetation, settlements, routes, and structures appropriate to the location's function. When human construction is present, give it a distinctive silhouette, clear access logic, crafted Victorian fantasy-steampunk materials, and a visible relationship to aerial travel, trade, defense, ceremony, or local work.

## Materials and Detail

Assign materials by function: timber, canvas, rope, brick, stone, plaster, iron, steel, brass, copper, colored glass, enamel, leather, sailcloth, and furnace glass. Show load-bearing structure, mechanism connections, circulation, and the interface between old craft and engineered systems.

Use trim, paint, livery, carved details, railings, rivets, valves, pulleys, braces, windows, doors, deck fittings, and stored equipment to establish ownership and purpose. Let colored or patterned glass, enamel panels, sculpted metalwork, repeated arches, and bespoke civic mechanisms create identity alongside functional detail. Keep surfaces clean, finished, and materially readable.
